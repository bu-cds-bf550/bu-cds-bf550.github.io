## The arc: what is unknown?

Three acts, organized by one escalating question — *what is unknown, and how would you know your
answer is right?*

- **Act I — the unknown is a number** (units 1–3). Build a process, estimate what went into it, put
  an honest error bar on it, and find out why real data is messier than your first model.
  *Knowing you're right:* you set the truth yourself.
- **Act II — the unknown is an answer you have examples of** (units 4–10). Supervised learning:
  generative models, evaluation, prediction with no probability story, then models that condition
  on what you measured. *Knowing you're right:* held-out data.
- **Act III — nobody gives you the answer** (units 11–12). Structure with no labels at all.
  *Knowing you're right:* there is no ground truth — stability, internal validation, and
  skepticism are all you have. The project opens during this act, and the term closes with the
  final exam.

## Fall 2026

The term is **38 class meetings**, and a **unit** is three of them, the sessions that carry one
problem set. A unit is not a calendar week. The term opens on a Wednesday and the holidays fall
unevenly, so units 2 to 4 run **Friday → Monday → Wednesday**: the topic and the new problem open
on Friday, you have the weekend with them, and Monday and Wednesday are working sessions. Class on
Mon Sep 28 was cancelled, and every meeting after it moved one place later. From unit 5 on, a unit
runs **Monday → Wednesday → Friday**: the topic and the new problem open on Monday, and Wednesday
and Friday are working sessions. Unit 5 has a fourth session on Tue Oct 13, the Monday-schedule
substitute day. On Oct 13, evaluation became two units, and unit 6 opens Wed Oct 14, so unit 6
runs **Wednesday → Friday → Monday**. Class on Wed Oct 21 is cancelled, and every meeting after it
moves one place later, so from unit 7 on a unit runs **Friday → Monday → Wednesday** again.
Thanksgiving recess falls inside unit 11, between its second and third sessions. No session of
units 1 to 12 is dropped. The two cancelled classes and unit 5's fourth session took their
meetings from the last unit, unit 13, which has one: the final exam.

**A problem set covers one unit's material and is assigned at the next unit's first session** —
after the chapter, the lectures, and the labs — **and is due at the first session of the unit after
that**, the same session the following set is handed out. So the set open during a unit is on the
*previous* unit's ideas: you build things after they have had time to settle rather than on the day
you were introduced to them.

The **Problem set** column below reads *due / assigned* for that unit's first session. All three of
a unit's sessions carry working time on the set assigned in the first of them, so you have the
room, the instructors and the TAs for the whole of a set's life.

**No problem set covers units 10 to 12**, and units 11 and 12 assign nothing. PS10, on unit 9's
material, opens at unit 10's first session, is open over the Thanksgiving recess, and is due
Mon Nov 30, in unit 11's last session. The project opens that same day and carries the rest of the
term. That is why there are **ten problem sets across thirteen units** rather than one per unit.
The final exam is Wed Dec 9, the last meeting.

Each unit number links to that unit's page, which carries the slides and the labs for its
meetings as they are posted — the material we work through in class, yours to finish at your own
pace afterwards.

## The final exam

| | When | Covers | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Final exam** | Wed Dec 9 | The whole course | Closed book, no AI, code reading |

There is one exam, and no midterm. It is a code-reading exam: you read snippets, say what they
do, and find where they fail. The snippets come from the work of the whole course, but the exam
asks about the code, not about the methods behind it. It is in class on Wed Dec 9, the last
meeting; **there is no exam during finals period.**

## The synthesis project

The course in miniature, and the culminating assessment: design a generative process for a
biological question you care about, then show what a method can and cannot recover from it. The same
thing you will have done ten times by then, on a problem you chose.
